User journey — circular.ie guided tour

From the moment someone sees the ad to the moment they come back. Curve shows the intended emotional arc, not measured data — use it to spot where the design has to work hardest to earn the next swipe.

🎯 Every stage below is annotated against the 30% engagement target and the accessibility bar this being a public-sector service requires.

Intended emotional arc across the journey

Illustrative design intent, not user-tested data — treat dips as design risks to test for, not measurements.

● Low drop-off risk ▲ Medium drop-off risk ■ High drop-off risk ♿ Accessibility / assistive-tech note
1

Sees the ad

Touchpoint: Instagram / TikTok / Meta feed ad, audience-segmented creative

"Repair café? Is that... free stuff?"

User is

  • Scrolling passively, low intent
  • Deciding in under a second whether to tap
Ad copy and alt text must carry the message alone — some users browse with images/autoplay off.
■ High risk

Why it matters: this is pure top-of-funnel; nothing here is fixable in-product, only through creative and targeting.

2

Lands on the tour

Touchpoint: mobile landing screen, often inside Instagram/TikTok's in-app browser

"Did that even load? ...oh, ok."

User is

  • Impatient — a slow or generic load loses them here
  • Deciding if this looks worth their thumb
In-app browsers often restrict local storage — don't rely on the browser remembering this visitor later.
▲ Medium risk

Why it matters: the segment-matched entry point (from the ad) should land them straight into a relevant card, not a generic homepage — this is the first real save-or-lose moment.

3

Learns a concept

Touchpoint: concept card ("what's a repair café?")

"Oh — I didn't actually know that was a thing."

User is

  • Genuinely learning, not just browsing a list
  • Building context for the group cards that follow
Progressive disclosure ("tell me more") must be a real, keyboard-reachable control — not hover-only.
● Low risk

Why it matters: this is the dwell-time, top-tier metric moment — the thing that separates a tour from a directory.

4

Discovers real groups

Touchpoint: group cards + moment/stat cards, repeating loop

"There's one near me? Heart that."

User is

  • Swiping, hearting, building a shortlist
  • At risk of fatigue if the loop runs too long before a payoff
Swipe must never be the only path (WCAG 2.5.1) — visible Previous/Next controls and a plain-list alternative are mandatory here.
▲ Medium risk

Why it matters: swipes and hearts are the countable, satisfying mid-tier metric — front-load the most save-worthy groups so a short session still feels like a win.

5

Reviews shortlist

Touchpoint: end-of-tour shortlist screen, "your circular Ireland"

"Oh nice, look at that — I built a little map."

User is

  • Feeling a small sense of ownership over the list
  • At risk of an empty, deflating screen if nothing was hearted
An empty state needs a real, non-judgemental message and a clear way back into the tour — not a dead end.
● Low risk

Why it matters: this is the pivot from consumption to a personal artifact — the thing worth sharing.

6

Emails / shares it

Touchpoint: lightweight email-to-self form, no account created

"...will this sign me up for spam?"

User is

  • Weighing a small trust decision — the only PII asked for in the whole flow
  • Deciding whether to also share the link with someone else
Labelled fields, inline (not colour-only) error messages, and a consent line kept separate from any newsletter opt-in.
■ High risk

Why it matters: this single field is the acquisition and share-out engine — keep the ask minimal and the wording honest about why it's needed.

7

Comes back

Touchpoint: the emailed board link, reopened days or weeks later, any device

"Oh — three new groups since I last looked."

User is

  • Returning with no login needed — the link itself is the "account"
  • Possibly sharing that same link onward to someone else
Anchor "what's new" to the board ID, not to device/browser memory, so it survives an in-app-browser reset.
● Low risk

Why it matters: repeat visits and referrals both come from this one link — it's the whole retention loop in a single URL.

Reading this map: the sentiment curve is design intent, drawn to flag two deliberate risk points — the ad-to-landing gap (pure funnel loss, outside the product) and the email ask (the one moment we request anything personal). Both deserve their own test before launch: real users on real in-app browsers, and a moderated read of the email-consent copy.

This map assumes the "merged" concept (editorial concept cards + experience-led swipe mechanic) discussed as the starting option — the editorial-led and experience-led variants would compress or expand stage 3–4 differently, worth mapping separately once the merged version is validated.
